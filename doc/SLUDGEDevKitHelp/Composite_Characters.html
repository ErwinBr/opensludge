Composite Characters

A character can be made of parts, each with a sprite of its own: legs, a torso, a head with its eyes, two arms. A rig, made with newRig, says what is attached to what; clips say how the parts move. A clip names the parts it moves, so it plays on any rig with parts of those names. 

sub heroRig () {
	var r = newRig ();
	addPart (r, "legs", "", 0, 0, 'hero_legs.duc', 0);
	addPart (r, "torso", "", 0, -250, 'hero_torso.duc', 0);
	addPart (r, "head", "torso", 3, -213, 'hero_head.duc', 0);
	addPart (r, "eyes", "head", 0, 0, 'hero_head.duc', 6);
	addPart (r, "arm", "torso", 70, -160, 'hero_arm.duc', 0);
	setPartDepth (r, "arm", -1);		# behind the torso
	return r;
}

sub wave () {
	var c = newClip (72);
	clipKey (c, "arm", PART_ROTATION, 0, 0);
	clipKey (c, "arm", PART_ROTATION, 24, -150, EASE_INOUT);
	clipKey (c, "arm", PART_ROTATION, 72, 0, EASE_INOUT);
	return c;
}

addCharacter (hero, 320, 400, heroCostume ());
setCharacterRig (hero, heroRig ());
pause (animate (hero, wave ()));

Where parts are

X is to the right and Y down, in pixels; angles are in degrees, clockwise. A part's origin is its sprite's hotspot, as the Sprite Bank Editor sets it: the part turns, scales and flips about it. Its x and y are where its origin sits, from the origin of the part it is attached to, which turns, scales and flips it too, so that a head turns with its torso and the eyes with the head. The character itself is the part called "", at its place, at its scale.

What clips do to parts

clipKey moves (PART_X, PART_Y), turns (PART_ROTATION), scales (PART_SCALE), flips (PART_FLIP), hides (PART_VISIBLE) and orders (PART_DEPTH) a part; clipSprite changes its sprite. Hiding a part hides what is attached to it; an empty frame (NOSPRITE) hides only the part's own sprite. Parts of a lower depth are drawn first, and parts of the same depth in the order they were added. Flipping the character's part, "", mirrors the whole character: a costume can face west with the clips that face east (see mirrorClip).

A clip may name parts that a rig doesn't have: those parts are left out. A sprite that its bank doesn't have shows the part's own sprite instead.

Layers

A character can play clips on layers of their own, over the animation of its costume, with playClip: eyes that blink, whatever the body does; a mouth that talks while the legs walk; breathing that adds to standing. A layer either sets what its clip keys, or adds to it. Over all layers, setPartProperty sets a part's property from the script, as a head that follows the mouse. A clip with nothing but an event, on a layer, is a timer: its event can play a blink at random times.

Costumes

Costumes of clips work as ever: standing, walking and talking, in each direction. Their clips can place the parts of each direction differently.

As one character

A character of parts is one character to everything else. The mouse is over it where it is over a pixel of any of its parts (with RECTANGULAR, see setCharacterExtra, anywhere in the box around all its parts), and not where the z-buffer is in front of it. pasteCharacter pastes all its parts as they are, freezing and snapshots keep it as they keep any character, and FIXTOSCREEN, NOZBUFFER, NOLIGHTMAP, transparency and colourise work for all its parts.
